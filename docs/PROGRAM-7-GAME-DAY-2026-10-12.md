Written Monday, October 12, 2026, for Jack Berno, on his word of the same day: the site becomes the game-day conditions desk, GoCheckMyWeather is the weather source, and the two properties promote each other. This document is the program the desks build from. It goes into both repositories as docs/PROGRAM-7-GAME-DAY-2026-10-12.md; the Sports desk carries sections 2 to 8, the Weather desk section 5. ## 1. The claim, in one line each

GoCheckMySports: every game that matters this week, the weather at kickoff, where to watch it, who is out, and the score after. GoCheckMyWeather: the weather at every stadium, hour by hour, with the alerts, and the app that tells you Saturday night what Sunday's kickoff will be. The tagline stays "Sports, checked." and the conditions line under every reading says "Conditions by GoCheckMyWeather, from the National Weather Service."

## 2. How it works

The schedule, the broadcasts, the venues and the scores come from the ESPN feed the desk already reads (the September 13 inventory confirmed the venue with its indoor flag and the broadcasts are carried cleanly). The lines stay as they are, as the book reported them. The inactives and designations stay as the fantasy layer built them.

The conditions come from one new Worker, conditions.gocheckmybrands.workers.dev, that lives in the Weather repository under workers/conditions, beside gauge-product, and serves both sites. For every stadium in the stadium table it reads the NWS hourly forecast for the stadium's coordinates (api.weather.gov, points then gridpoints hourly, the code the Weather site already has) and the active alerts over that point, caches each read for thirty minutes, and answers /game?id= with the kickoff slice: the hour before kickoff through the hour after the expected end, temperature and feel, wind speed, gust and direction, precipitation chance and type, sky, the alerts in force, and the stamp with its zone. It answers /stadium?id= with the full hourly line for the Weather site's page. It is deployed by Jack with the same line as gauge-product, and it is the one host besides the site's own that either site may read at runtime, named in each canary.

The stadium table is a file in the Weather repository, data/stadiums.json, served with the Worker: every NFL stadium and every FBS stadium with its ESPN venue id, name, city, coordinates, indoor or retractable flag, elevation, and the field's orientation in degrees from north where it has been entered. The flag and the coordinates come from the feed and the venue's own page; the orientation is hand-kept, the thirty NFL stadiums first, each row with its source and date; a stadium without an orientation prints wind speed and direction and no relative reading. Nothing in the table is guessed; a missing value prints as missing.

Wind against the field is the reading nobody else gives. From the wind direction and the field's orientation the Worker computes the component along the field and across it, and the page says it in words: "18 mph across the field", "12 mph down the field, gusting 20", with the arrow drawn on a small field graphic. Rain and lightning risk are the NWS precipitation chance and the alerts (a thunderstorm watch or warning over the stadium is printed in the NWS's own words with its expiry). A dome reads "indoor" and nothing else; a retractable reads "retractable roof" with the forecast beside it, since the roof call is the team's.

Where to watch is a rule table, not a guess: data/carriage.json in the Sports repository, hand-kept, one row per network and window with the streams that carry it (CBS games on Paramount+; NBC and Sunday night on Peacock; Thursday on Prime Video; Monday on the ESPN services; local affiliates on YouTube TV, Fubo, Hulu with Live TV and Sling where it carries the market; NFL+ on the phone; the out-of-market package on YouTube; the special-date games on their named services), each row with the service's own help page as its source and the date it was verified. A row older than thirty days prints "verify" instead of the service and the desk re-checks it in its weekly pass. The regional assignment of the 1:00 and 4:25 windows is linked to the source that publishes the maps, not copied.

The live board runs without deploys. On game days the Worker reads the ESPN scoreboard every sixty seconds from kickoff of the first game to the end of the last, caches it, and serves /live; the week board and the game pages read it from the browser and update their cards in place, with the stamp. The static build stays at its twelve a day and the Sunday deploy freeze stays, because the live reads do not need a deploy; the word "live" appears on the page only when the Worker's stamp is under three minutes old, and the card falls back to the last built score with its time when it is not.

The cadence of builds: Tuesday morning the week rolls (the new week's board, the previous week's finals in the record); Thursday morning the conditions report for the week is published and the first forecasts appear on the cards (the NWS hourly product reaches seven days, so a Sunday kickoff is in range from the Sunday before; the report waits for Thursday when the numbers settle); Saturday evening and Sunday morning the forecasts refresh on the cards; Sunday at 11:35 the inactives land; the Worker carries the rest of Sunday; Monday the finals.

The desk's writing: one piece a day, the conditions report, written from the Worker's numbers and the schedule, in the desk's plain words, Thursday through Sunday morning, and the Monday record. No recaps, no storylines, no lanes. The report names the games the weather will touch and says what the numbers are and what they usually mean (wind over 15 mph and the passing game, rain and the ball, cold and the kicking), with the NWS stamp; it is a fact page, which is the brand.

## 3. How it looks

The look that exists stays: the stadium band, the dark Ticket cards with the team-color bars, the green and red scores, the network chips, the phone tab bar, Inter and the mono. The program adds one element and changes the order.

The element is the conditions strip: one line on every game card, in GoCheckMyWeather's glyphs and colors so the reader sees the same weather on both sites and in the app. Four cells: temperature with the feel, wind as an arrow rotated to the direction with the speed, precipitation chance with the type, and the venue flag (indoor, retractable, open). The strip is muted when the game is indoors and bright when a number matters (wind 15 mph and over, precipitation 40 percent and over, a feel below 32 or above 90, an alert), and an alert turns the strip's edge rule red with the NWS's word on it. Under the strip, the credit in small type: "Conditions by GoCheckMyWeather, NWS, 9:10 AM ET", linking to the Weather site's stadium page.

The home page is the week board. The band carries "This week" with the day tabs (Thu, Sat, Sun, Mon) and the Sunday windows (early, 1:00, 4:05 and 4:25, night); the cards sit in kickoff order, each with the teams and records, the kickoff in ET, the network and the first stream as chips, the conditions strip, the line, and the inactives count once posted; a final shows the score in place of the kickoff and keeps its strip as the record. The first card of the day is the Ticket, the large one, and it is the game the weather touches most, or the night game when none does. Below the board sits the day's conditions report, then the team strip (every team as a colored chip to its page), then the footer. Nothing else on the home.

The game page is the product and its title is the search: "Bears at Packers: weather, where to watch, inactives. Sunday 1:00 PM ET." Above the fold: the Ticket with the teams and the kickoff; the where-to-watch block (the network, the streams as buttons with the price when the carriage table has it, the regional note with its link); the conditions block (the strip large, the hourly line from an hour before kickoff to an hour after the end as a small chart in the Weather site's chart style, wind against the field drawn on the field graphic, the alert if any, the credit and the link to the full forecast on the Weather site). Below: the inactives and designations when posted, the line as the book reported it, and after the game the score and the box. The tab bar on the phone becomes Week, Teams, Watch, Scores, More.

The team page is one per NFL team and the top forty programs: the next game's full card, the schedule with where to watch each game and the conditions once in range, the last result, the record and standing, and the team's conditions log (games in wind over 15, in rain, below freezing, this season). The market page is one per television market, "NFL on TV in Charleston this Sunday": the windows, the game the market's CBS and FOX carry as published by the source with its link, the night games, and the streams that carry each local affiliate in that market. Dark mode is designed as it is today.

Everything on the Weather side of the loop has the Weather site's look, not the Sports site's: the stadium page is a Weather page with a sports card at the top.

## 5. The promotion loop, GoCheckMyWeather's half

The Weather site gains stadium pages, one per NFL and FBS stadium at gocheckmyweather.com/stadiums/<slug>: the full hourly forecast through the week, the radar frame, the alerts, the sports card at the top with the next game at that stadium and its kickoff slice, and the link to the game page on Sports. They are Weather pages, in the Weather look, and they are the search door for "weather at Lambeau Field Sunday" and "Williams-Brice Stadium weather", which the Sports game pages do not compete with: the two pages answer different queries and link each other.

Jack's ruling of October 12, evening: the Weather app does not change. The loop runs one way, Sports to Weather: the credit and the link under every conditions block, the app badge on every game page, and the stadium pages on the Weather site. The push comes from the Sports site itself, as web push (iOS 16.4 and later when the site is added to the home screen, Android everywhere): the reader picks a team on the site, and the site sends one push the evening before that team's game with the one line that matters ("Wind 22 mph across the field at Lambeau at 1:00 PM"), opening the game page. One push per game, never more, no account, the subscription held on the device and the relay only; built in S-3.

The share card: every conditions report and every game page renders a square image on build (the Ticket with the strip and the wind arrow) for the desk to post; the week's weather board as one image on Thursday is the thing the fantasy and betting communities pass around, and it carries both marks.
